The Principal Upanishads: The Core Teachings of Non-Duality and Truth

Isha Upanishad
All this whatever exists in this changing universe is enveloped by the Divine. Therefore, find your delight in renunciation; do not covet the wealth of anyone. By performing selfless work in this world, one may wish to live a hundred years. There is no other way for you than this, whereby work does not cling to you.

He who perceives all beings in the Self alone, and the Self in all beings, does not hate anyone or turn away in sorrow. When to the sage all beings have become one with his own Self, what delusion, what sorrow can there be for him who sees that oneness?

The face of Truth is covered with a golden disc. Unveil it, O Sustainer, so that we who love the truth may behold its splendor. Lead us along the auspicious path to supreme good, O Divine Light, clearing away all deceptive sins from our path.

Katha Upanishad
The good is one thing; the pleasant is another. Both of these, with different aims, bind human beings. Of these two, it is well for the one who chooses the good; but the one who chooses the pleasant falls away from the true goal of life. The wise distinguish between the two, choosing the path of wisdom and virtue over the path of fleeting gratification.

Know the Self as the lord of the chariot, the body as the chariot itself, the intellect as the charioteer, and the mind as the reins. The senses are called the horses, and the sense objects are the roads along which they travel. When the Self is united with body, senses, and mind, the wise call it the experiencer.

He who is devoid of wisdom, whose mind is ever uncollected, and whose senses are uncontrolled, is like a charioteer with vicious and unruly horses. But he who is possessed of wisdom, whose mind is ever focused and senses subdued, reaches the supreme goal whence he is never thrown back into sorrow.

Arise! Awake! Approach the wise teachers and learn the truth. Sharp as the razor's edge, difficult to tread and hard to cross, is this path — so declare the sages. That which is soundless, touchless, formless, undecaying, tasteless, eternal, odorless, without beginning and without end, higher than the great, unchanging — by realizing That, one is freed from the jaws of mortality.

Mandukya Upanishad
All this world is the sacred syllable OM. Its explanation is this: the past, the present, and the future — all that was, is, and will be is OM. And whatever else is beyond the threefold division of time, that also is indeed OM. All this is Brahman; this Self is Brahman.

This Self has four quarters: the waking state (Vaishvanara) where outward knowledge experiences gross objects; the dreaming state (Taijasa) where inward knowledge experiences subtle creations of the mind; the deep sleep state (Prajna) where there are no desires and no dreams, unified in silent awareness and bliss; and the fourth state (Turiya).

Turiya is not that which is conscious of the subjective world, nor that which is conscious of the objective world, nor conscious of both. It is unseen, unapproachable by the senses, without distinct sign, unthinkable, unnameable, the essence of the consciousness of the one Self, in which the world ceases, tranquil, auspicious, non-dual (Shantam, Shivam, Advaitam). That is the true Self; That is to be realized.

Chandogya Upanishad
In the beginning, this universe was Being alone, one only without a second. From Being arose energy, from energy arose water, and from water arose all the forms of life. All living creatures have Being as their root, Being as their dwelling, and Being as their support.

As the bees make honey by collecting the juices of various trees and reduce those juices to a single unity, so do all creatures lose their separateness when they merge into pure Being.

"Bring me a fruit from that banyan tree," said the father to his son Shvetaketu. "Here it is, father." "Break it open; what do you see inside?" "Extremely tiny seeds, father." "Break open one of those seeds; what do you see inside?" "Nothing at all, father." The father said: "My dear boy, that subtle essence which you do not perceive is the very power from which this vast banyan tree grows. Believe this, my child. That subtle essence is the soul of this whole universe. That is the Truth. That is the Self. Tat Tvam Asi — Thou Art That."

Brihadaranyaka Upanishad
From the unreal, lead me to the Real. From darkness, lead me to Light. From death, lead me to Immortality (Asato ma sadgamaya, tamaso ma jyotirgamaya, mrityor ma amritam gamaya).

He who inhabits the earth, yet is within the earth, whom the earth does not know, whose body the earth is, and who controls the earth from within — He is your Self, the Inner Controller, the Immortal. He who inhabits all beings, who is within all beings, whom no being knows, whose body is all beings, and who rules all beings from within — He is your Self, the Antaryamin, the Immortal.

You cannot see the seer of seeing; you cannot hear the hearer of hearing; you cannot perceive the perceiver of perceiving; you cannot know the knower of knowing. That is your Self, which is within all. Everything else is of the perishable world.
