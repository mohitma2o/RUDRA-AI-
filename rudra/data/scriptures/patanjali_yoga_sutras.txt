Patanjali Yoga Sutras: The Science of Mind Mastery and Equanimity

Chapter 1: Samadhi Pada (Contemplation and Stillness)
Yoga is the stilling of the fluctuations and modifications of the mind (Yogas chitta vritti nirodha). When the whirlpools of thought settle into silence, then the Seer abides in its true, essential nature (Tada drashtuh svarupe avasthanam).

At other times, when the mind is turbulent, the observer identifies with the fleeting thoughts, taking joy and sorrow as their own. The fluctuations of the mind are fivefold: right perception, misconception, imagination, sleep, and memory. These are stilled through continuous practice (abhyasa) and non-attachment (vairagya).

Practice is the sustained effort to secure and maintain calmness of the mind. Non-attachment is the conscious mastery of desire for visible and promised pleasures. By cultivating an attitude of friendliness toward the happy, compassion toward the suffering, joy toward the virtuous, and equanimity toward the unvirtuous, the mind attains undisturbed peace and clarity.

Chapter 2: Sadhana Pada (The Practice of Action and Discipline)
Kriya Yoga consists of self-discipline (tapas), self-study and contemplation (svadhyaya), and surrender to the divine supreme reality (Ishvara pranidhana). Its purpose is to weaken the afflictions (kleshas) and cultivate meditative absorption.

The root afflictions of the human soul are ignorance of true nature (avidya), egotism (asmita), clinging to pleasure (raga), aversion to pain (dvesha), and the clinging to bodily life out of fear of death (abhinivesha). Ignorance is the breeding ground for all other afflictions; it causes one to mistake the transient for the eternal, the impure for the pure, pain for pleasure, and the non-Self for the Self.

The eight limbs of Yoga (Ashtanga Yoga) are:
1. Yama (Ethical restraints): Non-violence in thought, word, and deed (Ahimsa); truthfulness (Satya); non-stealing (Asteya); conservation of vital energy (Brahmacharya); and non-covetousness (Aparigraha).
2. Niyama (Observances): Purity and cleanliness (Saucha); contentment with what is (Santosha); self-discipline (Tapas); study of spiritual wisdom (Svadhyaya); and devotion to the Divine (Ishvara pranidhana).
3. Asana: Steady and comfortable posture.
4. Pranayama: Regulation of the vital breath.
5. Pratyahara: Withdrawal of the senses from external distractions.
6. Dharana: Focused concentration of the mind on a single point.
7. Dhyana: Unbroken flow of meditative awareness.
8. Samadhi: Absorption into pure consciousness where the observer, the observed, and the act of meditation become one.

When non-violence (Ahimsa) is firmly established in a person, all hostility ceases in their presence. In the presence of truthfulness (Satya), actions bear righteous fruit. Through contentment (Santosha), unexcelled happiness is gained.
